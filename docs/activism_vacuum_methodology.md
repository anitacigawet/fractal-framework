# Activism Power Vacuum Identification Methodology

## Introduction

This document outlines the methodology for identifying "activism power vacuums" across counties in the United States. An activism power vacuum represents an area where there is a significant need for community activism but insufficient organized efforts to address the issue. By identifying these vacuums, we can create targeted templates that empower local citizens to establish effective advocacy organizations modeled after the SaveMohave project structure.

## Core Principles

1. **Local Relevance**: Issues must directly impact the specific county and its residents
2. **Actionable Opportunity**: Problems must be addressable through organized community activism
3. **Resource Viability**: Solutions must be achievable with reasonable local resources
4. **Structural Applicability**: Issues should map to the organizational framework established in the SaveMohave model
5. **Community Empowerment**: Focus on issues where informed citizen action can drive meaningful change

## Identification Process

### Step 1: County Profile Analysis

For each county, establish a baseline profile including:

- **Demographic Characteristics**: Population size, density, urban/rural classification
- **Economic Indicators**: Major industries, unemployment rates, poverty levels
- **Geographic Features**: Natural resources, environmental assets, climate vulnerabilities
- **Governance Structure**: County government type, regulatory frameworks
- **Community Resources**: Existing nonprofit organizations, community groups, educational institutions

### Step 2: Issue Category Screening

Screen each county for potential issues across these categories:

1. **Environmental Concerns**
   - Water quality and access (similar to SaveMohave)
   - Air quality issues
   - Land use and conservation
   - Waste management and pollution
   - Climate change impacts and resilience

2. **Public Health Challenges**
   - Healthcare access disparities
   - Mental health resource gaps
   - Substance abuse prevention and treatment
   - Food security and nutrition
   - Public health infrastructure

3. **Economic Development Needs**
   - Small business support
   - Workforce development
   - Affordable housing
   - Transportation infrastructure
   - Digital divide and connectivity

4. **Social Justice Issues**
   - Educational equity
   - Criminal justice reform
   - Immigrant rights and integration
   - Homelessness and poverty reduction
   - Accessibility for people with disabilities

5. **Civic Engagement Gaps**
   - Voter participation and education
   - Government transparency and accountability
   - Youth leadership development
   - Community planning participation
   - Disaster preparedness and response

### Step 3: Vacuum Assessment Criteria

For each potential issue area, evaluate:

1. **Problem Severity**
   - Scale: 1-5 (1 = minor concern, 5 = critical crisis)
   - Based on quantitative data where available and qualitative assessment

2. **Existing Response Adequacy**
   - Scale: 1-5 (1 = robust existing response, 5 = no organized response)
   - Measures the gap between need and current activism

3. **Community Awareness**
   - Scale: 1-5 (1 = high awareness, 5 = minimal awareness)
   - Assesses need for education vs. action mobilization

4. **Regulatory Framework**
   - Scale: 1-5 (1 = strong regulations exist, 5 = regulatory vacuum)
   - Identifies where policy advocacy is needed

5. **Resource Requirements**
   - Scale: 1-5 (1 = minimal resources needed, 5 = extensive resources required)
   - Helps prioritize achievable initiatives

### Step 4: Vacuum Identification

Calculate a Vacuum Score for each issue area:
- **Vacuum Score** = (Problem Severity × 0.3) + (Existing Response Adequacy × 0.3) + (Community Awareness × 0.2) + (Regulatory Framework × 0.1) + (Resource Requirements × 0.1)

Interpretation:
- **4.0-5.0**: Critical activism vacuum - immediate opportunity
- **3.0-3.9**: Significant activism vacuum - strong opportunity
- **2.0-2.9**: Moderate activism vacuum - potential opportunity
- **1.0-1.9**: Minor activism vacuum - limited opportunity

### Step 5: Template Matching

For identified vacuums scoring 3.0 or higher:

1. Map the issue to the SaveMohave project structure
2. Identify which components require significant adaptation
3. Determine county-specific resources and stakeholders
4. Assess potential for community engagement and impact
5. Prioritize based on feasibility and potential impact

## Implementation Strategy

For each county, this methodology will be applied to identify the top 1-3 activism vacuums that represent the most promising opportunities for community action. These will then be developed into county-specific templates following the SaveMohave structural framework but adapted for the particular issue and local context.

The resulting templates will provide a comprehensive roadmap for local activists to establish effective advocacy organizations addressing the most pressing needs in their communities.

## Example Application

**County**: Jefferson County, Colorado
**Population**: 582,881
**Key Characteristics**: Suburban/urban mix, high education levels, significant natural areas

**Top Identified Vacuum**: Wildfire Mitigation and Forest Management Advocacy
- Problem Severity: 4.5 (High risk of catastrophic wildfire)
- Existing Response Adequacy: 3.8 (Some government programs but limited community organization)
- Community Awareness: 2.5 (Moderate awareness but limited action)
- Regulatory Framework: 3.0 (Some regulations exist but enforcement and coordination gaps)
- Resource Requirements: 2.0 (Moderate resources needed)
- **Vacuum Score**: 3.53 - Significant activism vacuum

This would be developed into a county-specific template following the SaveMohave structure but focused on wildfire mitigation advocacy instead of water rights.
